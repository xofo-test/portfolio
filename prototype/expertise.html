Engineering principles

How I make architecture decisions.

Three rules that have survived four companies and two rewrites.

Boring where it counts

Money movement, identity and data storage get proven, well-understood technology. Novelty is spent on the product surface, where it earns attention.

Design for change

Clear boundaries, versioned contracts and reversible migrations. The measure of a design is how cheaply the next requirement can be added.

Measure what you ship

Every roadmap item names the metric it moves. For AI that means golden sets and citation accuracy, not demos that look impressive once.

Isolation

The AI path never writes to the ledger directly. It proposes structured output to a domain service that validates and records it, so a model failure cannot corrupt money.

Tenancy

Every table carries a tenant key with row-level security, and every vector query filters by tenant before similarity search — never after.

Failure modes

Timeouts, budget guards, fallback models and a circuit breaker around each provider. Degrading to search-only is an explicit, tested state.

Technical leadership

Seniority is mostly about making other people effective.

I have led squads of three to six engineers, hired four, and mentored six. My teams keep written decision records, review designs before code, and treat incidents as learning rather than blame. I still take on-call shifts and still review pull requests — the engineering is not a phase I moved past.

Talk about a role
  1. 01

    Design reviews before code

    One-page design docs with alternatives considered; review within 48 hours so momentum is never the casualty.

  2. 02

    Review culture

    Small PRs, reviewed within a day, with review guidelines that focus on correctness and maintainability over style.

  3. 03

    Incident learning

    Blameless retrospectives with tracked action items; two provider outages became runbook improvements before the next one.

  4. 04

    Growth that sticks

    Explicit growth plans, pairing rotations and ownership of a production surface from week one.
